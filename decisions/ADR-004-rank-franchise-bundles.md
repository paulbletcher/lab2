# ADR-004: Require three scored films per bundle and negotiate The Lord of the Rings first

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Decision owner:** Paul Bletcher, analyst

## Context

Kwame is negotiating for bundles that can include weaker films alongside
hits. The candidate list includes only one scored film from the Jaws
collection, at 67.24× reported box-office revenue per dollar of production
budget. The shortlist needs enough observed films to compare performance
within a collection and a way to show its weaker entries.

## Decision

I require at least three films with usable revenue-to-budget ratios in each
release window, rank qualifying collections by median ratio, and show
their lowest observed ratio. I recommend opening talks for The Lord of the
Rings because it ranks second across all years and first from 2000 onward,
and its weakest observed film scored 9.37×.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Allow collections with two scored films

- **How it would work:** Lower the eligibility threshold to two films
  with usable ratios in each release window.
- **Why it is attractive:** Star Wars would qualify from 2000 onward
  with two scored films. Indiana Jones would still fall short with one.
- **Why rejected:** With two films, the median is the average of both
  ratios, so one exceptional title can lift it substantially. Three
  films provide an observed middle result and let Kwame see how far the
  weakest film sits below it.

### Alternative B: Open with the all-years winner

- **How it would work:** Negotiate for Star Wars first because its
  all-years median of 13.40× leads the qualifying bundles.
- **Why it is attractive:** Star Wars has the highest all-years median
  and six scored films in that window.
- **Why rejected:** Four of those six films predate 2000, leaving Star Wars
  below the three-film minimum in the later window. Its lowest observed
  ratio is 5.41×, compared with 9.37× for The Lord of the Rings.

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** Kwame can compare the typical and weakest observed
  film in each shortlisted bundle. Across all years, the lowest ratios
  are 9.37× for The Lord of the Rings, 5.41× for Star Wars, and 4.25×
  for Indiana Jones.
- **What this costs:** The cutoff excludes collections with only one
  or two scored films. The lowest observed ratio also depends on which
  films were in the candidate list.
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** Kwame could pass over an
  attractive two-film offer or pay a premium for a bundle that excludes
  some of the films supporting its ranking. Confirm the exact titles
  and rights before setting a price ceiling.
- **What would make me revisit this:** If the studio offers a two-film
  package, evaluate those two titles and the quoted fee directly. If
  the offer for The Lord of the Rings excludes any of the three analyzed
  films or adds other titles, reassess the package using the offered list.

## What this survives

- **One hit dominates a sparsely represented collection.** Jaws scores
  67.24×, but its single observed film falls below the shortlist minimum.
- **Yaw asks to focus on releases from 2000 onward.** The Lord of the
  Rings still qualifies with all three analyzed films and leads that
  window with an 11.73× median.
